Project Idea: Decentralized multi-agent payload transport

= System Description

== Chosen Nonlinear System

We study a cooperative manipulation system consisting of two quadrupedal robots
(Unitree Go2), each equipped with a four-joint arm (Unitree D1) whose
end-effector is a flat contact pad. The two robots must coordinate to
*pinch, lift, and transport* a rigid payload through physical contact forces
alone --- without any rigid mechanical coupling or inter-robot communication.
This system is motivated by real-world logistics and construction scenarios
where large, ungraspable objects must be moved by teams of legged robots
capable of traversing unstructured terrain.

*Modeling assumptions.* The Unitree Go2 has 12 leg joints (3 per leg $times$ 4
legs). We do not include these as generalized coordinates in the high-level
model for two reasons. First, the leg joints will be governed by a pre-trained, frozen
low-level locomotion policy that accepts base velocity commands and outputs
leg joint targets. From the perspective of the high-level manipulation task,
the legs form an inner feedback loop and the base behaves as a
velocity-controlled rigid body; the relevant state is therefore the base pose,
not the individual leg configurations. Second, including all 12 leg joints per
robot would expand the zero dynamics to over 60 dimensions, which is
intractable for symbolic analysis. This simplification is standard in the
legged manipulation literature. We retain the full 4-DOF arm (Unitree D1) since
arm joint angles directly determine end-effector contact forces and are the
primary actuated degrees of freedom in the manipulation task.

The configuration of the full system is:

$
q = (q_1^b, theta_1^a, q_2^b, theta_2^a, p) in RR^26
$

where:
- $q_r^b = (x_r, y_r, z_r, phi_r, theta_r, psi_r) in RR^6$ is the
  floating-base pose of robot $r$,
- $theta_r^a = (theta_(r,1)^a, theta_(r,2)^a, theta_(r,3)^a, theta_(r,4)^a)
  in RR^4$ are the four revolute arm joint angles of robot $r$,
- $p = (p_x, p_y, p_z, alpha, beta, gamma) in RR^6$ is the 6-DOF payload
  pose.

The total system has $n = 10 + 10 + 6 = 26$ degrees of freedom. The control
inputs are the arm joint torques $u = (tau_1^a, tau_2^a) in RR^8$ (4 torques
per robot), and the contact wrenches $F = (F_1, F_2) in RR^12$ act as
constraint forces at the two end-effector contact frames. The leg joints are
not control inputs at this level; base velocity commands are treated as
outputs of the high-level policy passed to the low-level locomotion controller.

== Nonlinear Differential Equations

The equations of motion are derived from the Euler--Lagrange equations for
the Lagrangian $cal(L)(q, dot(q)) = T(q, dot(q)) - P(q)$, where the total
kinetic energy is:

$
T = 1/2 dot(q)^T D(q) dot(q)
$

with block-diagonal inertia matrix:

$
D(q) = mat(
  D_11(q_1), 0, 0;
  0, D_22(q_2), 0;
  0, 0, D_(o o)(p)
) in RR^(26 times 26)
$

Each robot block captures the floating-base inertia and the arm's
contribution through its end-effector Jacobian $J_r^("ee")$:

$
D_(r r)(q_r) = mat(
  m_r I_3 + m_e (J_r^"ee")^T J_r^"ee"|_"pos", dots.c;
  dots.c, W_r^T I_r W_r + m_e (J_r^"ee")^T J_r^"ee"|_"rot"
) in RR^(10 times 10)
$
where $W_r(phi_r, theta_r) in RR^(3 times 3)$ is the kinematic mapping from
ZYX Euler angle rates to body-frame angular velocity:

$
W_r = mat(
  1, 0, -sin theta_r;
  0, cos phi_r, cos theta_r sin phi_r;
  0, -sin phi_r, cos theta_r cos phi_r
)
$

Note that $W_r$ becomes singular at $theta_r = plus.minus pi\/2$ (gimbal
lock), so the relative degree analysis in the following section is restricted
to the domain $theta_r in (-pi\/2, pi\/2)$.

The payload inertia block is:

$
D_(o o)(p) = mat(m_o I_3, 0; 0, W_o^T I_o W_o) in RR^(6 times 6)
$

The potential energy is:

$
P(q) = m_1 g z_1 + m_2 g z_2
      + m_e g z_1^"ee"(q_1) + m_e g z_2^"ee"(q_2)
      + m_o g p_z
$

yielding the gravity vector $G(q) = (partial P \/ partial q)^T in RR^26$.

Applying the Euler--Lagrange equations with holonomic contact constraints
(following Lecture 26), the full equations of motion are:

$
D(q) dot.double(q) + C(q, dot(q)) dot(q) + G(q) = B u + J_kappa (q)^T F
$

where:
- $C(q, dot(q)) in RR^(26 times 26)$ contains the Coriolis and centripetal
  terms, including the $omega times (I_o omega)$ Euler term for the
  rotating payload,
- $B in RR^(26 times 8)$ is the actuation matrix selecting the eight arm
  joint torques (4 per robot); it has zero rows corresponding to the
  floating-base and payload DOF, reflecting the underactuation of the system,
- $J_kappa(q) in RR^(12 times 26)$ is the stacked contact Jacobian
  enforcing the 6-DOF end-effector constraints for each robot:

$
kappa_r (q_r, p) = 0, quad r = 1, 2
$

These constraints require that each arm's end-effector position and
orientation coincide with the designated contact frame $attach(T, tl: b, br: c f, tr: (r))$
on the payload surface. Differentiating twice gives the constraint on
accelerations:


$
J_kappa (q) dot.double(q) = -dot(J)_kappa (q, dot(q)) dot(q)
$

and the contact forces $F$ are the Lagrange multipliers enforcing this,
subject to the friction-cone admissibility constraint:

$
F_r in cal(A C)(F_r) = {F_r in RR^6 : F_n^((r)) > 0, norm(F_t^((r))) <= mu F_n^((r))}
$

where $F_n^((r))$ is the normal force component (positive = pushing into the
payload surface, since there is no adhesion) and $F_t^((r))$ is the tangential
force component bounded by the friction coefficient $mu$.

Written as a first-order control-affine system with state
$x = (q, dot(q)) in RR^52$:

$
dot(x) = underbrace(
  vec(dot(q), -D(q)^(-1)(C(q,dot(q))dot(q) + G(q))),
  f(x)
)
+
underbrace(
  mat(0_(26 times 8); D(q)^(-1) B),
  g_u (x)
) u
+
underbrace(
  mat(0_(26 times 12); D(q)^(-1) J_kappa(q)^T),
  g_F (x)
) F
$


















== Feedback Linearization and Zero Dynamics 
=== Output selection 

We choose the 6-DOF payload pose error as the output, encoding the control
objective of tracking a desired payload trajectory $p_d(t)$:

$
y(q, t) = mat(
  p_(x y z)(q) - p_d^(x y z)(t);
  e_R (q, t)
) in RR^6
$

where $p_(x y z)(q) in RR^3$ is the payload position extracted from $q$, and
$e_R(q, t) = "Log"(R_d^T R_o(q)) in RR^3$ is the orientation error via the
matrix logarithm, with $R_o(q) in S O(3)$ the payload rotation and $R_d(t)$
the desired rotation. This map is smooth and well-defined on the domain
$norm(e_R) < pi$, i.e., excluding $180 degree$ rotations. This is a virtual constraint output of the form
$y(q, t) = y_a (q) - y_d (t)$, where $y_a(q)$ encodes the actual payload pose
and $y_d(t)$ the desired trajectory.

=== Relative Degree

We determine the relative degree by differentiating $y$ along solutions of the
equations of motion. Since $y$ depends on $q$ but not $dot(q)$, the first
time derivative is:

$
dot(y) = (partial y) / (partial q) dot(q) = J_y (q) dot(q) - dot(y)_d (t)
$

where $J_y(q) = (partial y_a) / (partial q) in RR^(6 times 26)$ is the
output Jacobian. The control input $u$ does not appear in $dot(y)$, so
$L_g h(x) equiv 0$.

Differentiating a second time and substituting the equations of motion:

$
dot.double(y) = underbrace(
  dot(J)_y (q, dot(q)) dot(q) + J_y (q) (- D(q)^(-1) (C(q, dot(q)) dot(q) + G(q))) - dot.double(y)_d,
  L_f^2 y(q, dot(q), t)
)
+ underbrace(
  J_y (q) D(q)^(-1) B,
  cal(G)(q, dot(q))
) u
$

The *decoupling matrix* is (following Lecture 18, eq. 18.24 and Lecture 21,
eq. 21.7):

$
cal(G)(q, dot(q)) = L_g L_f y = J_y (q) D(q)^(-1) B in RR^(6 times 8)
$

This matrix has 6 rows (output dimension) and 8 columns (control input
dimension), so the system is *non-square*. We therefore consider
$cal(G) cal(G)^T in RR^(6 times 6)$ and use the Moore--Penrose pseudoinverse
$cal(G)^+ = cal(G)^T (cal(G) cal(G)^T)^(-1)$ for the feedback linearizing
controller, which exists whenever $cal(G)$ has full row rank (rank 6). This
holds when the arm Jacobians $J_r^"ee"$ are non-singular, i.e., away from
kinematic singularities of the D1 arm, and when $theta_r in (-pi\/2, pi\/2)$
to avoid gimbal lock in $W_r$.

Since $u$ does not appear in $dot(y)$ but does appear in $dot.double(y)$, the
system has vector relative degree $arrow(gamma) = (2, 2, 2, 2, 2, 2)$, i.e.,
vector relative degree 2 for all six output components.

=== Feedback Linearizing Controller

Under vector relative degree 2, the output dynamics take the form (Lecture 18,
eq. 18.25; Lecture 21, eq. 21.5):

$
dot.double(y) = L_f^2 y(q, dot(q), t) + cal(G)(q, dot(q)) u
$

Following Proposition 21.1, we feedback linearize by choosing:

$
u^* = cal(G)^+ (q, dot(q)) (-L_f^2 y(q, dot(q), t) + v)
$

where $v in RR^6$ is the new auxiliary input, yielding $dot.double(y) = v$.
To exponentially stabilize the output error, we pick a linear PD law on the
error coordinates $eta = (y, dot(y)) in RR^12$:

$
v = -K_P y - K_D dot(y)
$

with $K_P, K_D in RR^(6 times 6)$ chosen positive definite such that:

$
A_"cl" = mat(0_6, I_6; -K_P, -K_D) in RR^(12 times 12)
$

is Hurwitz (all eigenvalues have strictly negative real part). The closed-loop
output dynamics then satisfy:

$
dot(eta) = A_"cl" eta ==> eta(t) -> 0 "exponentially as" t -> infinity
$

This drives $y(q(t), t) -> 0$, i.e., the payload tracks $p_d(t)$
exponentially from any initial condition in the valid domain.


=== Zero Dynamics

When $y equiv 0$ and $dot(y) equiv 0$, the system is constrained to the
*zero dynamics surface*:

$
cal(Z) = {(q, dot(q)) : y(q, t) = 0, dot(y)(q, dot(q), t) = 0}
$

Following Lecture 19 and the MIMO normal form of Lecture 21, the zero dynamics
evolve on $cal(Z)$ and have dimension:

$
dim(cal(Z)) = 2n - 2k = 2(26) - 2(6) = 40
$

where $n = 26$ is the number of generalized coordinates and $k = 6$ is the
output dimension. The zero dynamics coordinates $z in RR^40$ capture the
internal degrees of freedom of the system that are not constrained by the
output: specifically, the arm joint configurations of both robots and their
rates, and the floating-base poses and rates, subject to $y = 0$.

Physically, the zero dynamics describe how the robot configurations evolve
while the payload tracks the desired trajectory perfectly. Stability of the
zero dynamics is what determines whether the robots maintain a consistent,
non-degenerate formation around the payload --- or whether arm joint angles or
base poses drift to singularities.

*Symbolic derivation.* Following Lecture 19, the zero dynamics coordinates
$z(x)$ must satisfy $D z(x) g_u(x) equiv 0$. For our system this reduces to:

$
(partial z) / (partial q) D(q)^(-1) B equiv 0
$

This is a system of 40 partial differential equations in 52 unknowns, which is
not tractable to solve symbolically for a 26-DOF system. We therefore assess
zero dynamics stability via simulation.
